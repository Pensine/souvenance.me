How to own your AI memory
(instead of renting it)

If you use an AI assistant daily, a second asset is quietly accumulating next to your conversations: its model of you. Your preferences, projects, relationships, patterns of decision. It makes every answer better — and it is precisely what will make leaving impossible.

Step 1 — Audit what the labs already hold

Open your assistant's memory panel (ChatGPT: Settings → Personalization; Claude: project knowledge and memory settings, where available) and read what has been written about you. Then request a full data export from each provider. Two things usually surprise people: how much is there, and how much of it they never explicitly said.

Step 2 — Understand the export gap

Data exports return your conversations. The distilled profile — the part that actually personalizes the product — typically doesn't travel in any reusable form. As of 2026, no major lab offers a portable, interoperable memory. That's not an oversight; accumulated context is the moat. A 2026 study of ChatGPT memories found 96% were created unilaterally by the system, about half containing psychological inferences.

Frontier intelligence is becoming a commodity. Compute is rented; context is owned — by someone. The question is by whom.

Step 3 — Own the substrate

The durable fix isn't a better export — it's inverting the architecture: the memory lives with you, and the AI visits. Concretely:

  • an append-only event log (PostgreSQL) on your VPS — every fact immutable, timestamped, re-processable by any future engine;
  • nightly consolidation run by the AI you already pay for (a Claude subscription can drive it headless), producing searchable memories and a temporal knowledge graph;
  • served back to your assistant through MCP, the open protocol — so the model, the interface, even the vendor stay swappable.

Step 4 — Make capture effortless

Any system that requires discipline dies in three weeks. Memory must be the byproduct of tools you use because they're useful: a share-sheet shortcut (two taps from a WhatsApp voice note to the archive), a two-minute daily voice log, silent perceptors for your calendar. Never a journaling chore.

Step 5 — Keep the originals

Store the actual audio, photos and videos forever — encrypted, on disk you control. Transcripts are catalog cards for search; the file is for replay. In ten years you won't want the summary of the morning before the race. You'll want the voice, the breathing, the doubt.

The build-vs-install question

Everything above is buildable from open parts (Postgres + pgvector, WhisperX, BGE-M3, MCP). If you'd rather not assemble it yourself, Souvenance is exactly this architecture as a finished, self-hosted product — open-source engine, one-time install kit, no subscription, and an archive designed to outlive it.
