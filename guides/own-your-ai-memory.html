HOW TO OWN YOUR AI MEMORY (INSTEAD OF RENTING IT)

If you use an AI assistant daily, a second asset is quietly accumulating next to your conversations: its model of you. Your preferences, projects, relationships, patterns of decision. It makes every answer better — and it is precisely what will make leaving impossible.

01 · AUDIT WHAT THE LABS ALREADY HOLD

Open your assistant's memory panel — ChatGPT: Settings → Personalization → Memory; Claude: Settings → Memory, where available — and actually read what has been written about you. Then request a full data export from each provider. Two things usually surprise people: how much is there, and how much of it they never explicitly said.

02 · UNDERSTAND THE EXPORT GAP

Data exports return your conversations. The distilled profile — the part that actually personalises the product — typically doesn't travel in any reusable form. As of 2026, no major lab offers portable, interoperable memory.

That's not an oversight: accumulated context is the moat. A 2026 study of ChatGPT memories found 96% were created unilaterally by the system, about half containing psychological inferences. Frontier intelligence is becoming a commodity; compute is rented and context is owned — by someone. The only open question is by whom.

03 · OWN THE SUBSTRATE

The durable fix isn't a better export — it's inverting the architecture: the memory lives with you, and the AI visits. Concretely:

  • An append-only event log (PostgreSQL) on your own server — every fact immutable, timestamped, re-processable by any future engine. No UPDATE, no DELETE: the past is not editable.
  • Nightly consolidation run by whichever AI backend you choose — a CLI on a subscription you already pay for, an API key, or a fully local model via Ollama.
  • Served back to your assistant through MCP, the open protocol — so the model, the interface, even the vendor stay swappable.

One database, no queue, no vector service: boring, proven, still readable in twenty years.

04 · MAKE CAPTURE EFFORTLESS

Any system that requires discipline dies in three weeks. Memory must be the byproduct of tools you use because they're useful: a share-sheet shortcut (two taps from a voice note to the archive), a two-minute daily voice log, silent perceptors for your calendar. Never a journaling chore.

05 · KEEP THE ORIGINALS

Store the actual audio, photos and videos forever, on disk you control. Transcripts are only catalogue cards: they let you find, the recording lets you relive. In ten years, the summary of a moment will be worth far less than thirty seconds of your own voice describing it.

THE ONE RULE THAT MAKES IT LAST

Whatever you build, make it never invite you back. No notifications, no streaks, no open questions engineered for retention. A memory system that competes for your attention has already failed at its purpose, which is to give you more life outside the screen — not more time inside it.
